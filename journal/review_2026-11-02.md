# AlgoVision review: what has worked so far - 2026-11-02

What in the daily report is actually relevant on the evidence so far: 39 dated reports, 2026-09-05 to 2026-10-29. Every name is measured from its first appearance in a table, entry at the next open, over two horizons: 20 bars ahead, and to the current close ('to date': every name ever listed, each over its own holding length), against the beaten-down basket of that day (the fair benchmark for rules that only buy beaten-down stocks) and against the SPY. Every table and condition gets a verdict per horizon. The verdicts are fixed rules (at the end), not judgement: a table 'works' only with 20+ names and a t of 2 or more on the basket excess; a condition 'separates' only with 12+ names on each side and a t of 2 or more on the difference. The windows overlap and the sample is a few weeks in one regime, so the t overstates the evidence; the thresholds are strict on purpose. The review changes no rule: the ten-year backtests decide what enters the report.

## Method

Source: the tables of the dated reports (journal/report_<date>.md) and the journal's mark_to_market.csv. Two horizons: 20 bars after the listing, and to the current close (every name, over its own holding length; an uneven horizon, so it summarises where things stand now rather than the result of a fixed rule). Table verdict: 20+ names with a return at the horizon; 'works' when the mean excess over the beaten-down basket is positive with t >= 2; 'not working' when negative with t <= -2; 'noise' otherwise. Condition verdict: 12+ names on each side, Welch t on the difference in basket excess; |t| >= 2 names the better side. Journal rule: 10+ closed trades; 'on track' when the mean is positive and the hit rate is within 10 points of the expectation. Survivorship bias, overlapping windows and a single regime apply throughout. Systematic screens and a forward test, not investment advice.
